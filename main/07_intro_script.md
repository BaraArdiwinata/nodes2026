# Intro Script: "Jas Merah" (slides 1-5, about 4 min)

Word-for-word opening lines. The same text is in the PowerPoint speaker notes. Speak slowly; pause after the quote.
Order: hook (quote → problem → question) first, then introduce ourselves.

## Slide 1: Jas Merah
> [Pause 2-3 seconds.] "Jas Merah. Jangan sekali-kali meninggalkan sejarah.
>
> In Indonesia we grow up with this phrase. It comes from our first president, Soekarno, and it means: never abandon history. Every Indonesian student knows it by heart."

## Slide 2: History class was a list
> "But honestly? For most of us, history class meant memorising lists: kings, kingdoms, years. Memorise for the exam, forget by next semester.
>
> And lists are easy to forget. The interesting part of history was never the list. It's the connections: who was whose child, who married into which dynasty, who took the throne next."

## Slide 3: One family, three kingdoms
> "Here's one story. Rara Santang was a princess of Pajajaran, a Hindu-Sundanese kingdom. Her son, Sunan Gunung Jati, helped Islam take root in Cirebon. His son, Maulana Hasanuddin, founded the Sultanate of Banten.
>
> Three generations, three kingdoms, and a religious turning point for West Java. In a textbook these sit in three different chapters. In a graph, they're just one path."

## Slide 4: The question
> "So our question was simple: can we make history explorable, instead of memorised? And, if we're lucky, could you ask it questions the way you'd ask a teacher?
>
> That question is what this talk is about. But first, who are we?"

## Slide 5: Who we are
> "Hi everyone! We're Bara and Annisa, Information Systems students from ITS, Institut Teknologi Sepuluh Nopember, in Surabaya, Indonesia.
>
> Our project is NusantaraGraph. Nusantara is the old name for the Indonesian archipelago. We tried to turn the family trees of Indonesia's pre-colonial kingdoms into a graph you can explore, and even talk to.
>
> We want to be upfront: this is a learning journey, not a production system. We'll show you what we tried, what broke, and what we learned."

## Closing line (slide 17), echoes the hook
> "Jas Merah says never abandon history. Graphs, and agents that query them honestly, make it a little harder to forget. Terima kasih."
